Summary

The technical basics are solid. Every page returns 200, points to itself as the canonical version, is in the sitemap, renders on the server and is open to every search and AI crawler. The writing is clear and specific, and competitor prices are dated and sourced. What holds the site back is the wrong page type for the main keyword, missing trust signals, and a slow homepage on mobile.

Before the sale ends on 27 November

Make the price markup and sale copy roll over cleanly

  • The homepage price markup (Offer schema) has the $49 price but no end date or availability. After the sale it would advertise $99 in stock while checkout is closed until 30 Nov. Add priceValidUntil: 2026-11-27, then switch to PreOrder with availabilityStarts: 2026-11-30. Drive it from currentOffer() in lib/pricing.ts.
  • Check the Black Friday text on the homepage, /pricing and /roadmap changes on its own, including the roadmap sentence "Everyone who buys by Black Friday…". State a concrete price for future updates.
  • Optional: a sitewide sale bar. The offer is only above the fold on the homepage today.

Top issues

  1. 1

    Three pages compete for "loom alternative", and none matches what Google shows

    About 78% of results are roundups comparing many tools. You have a landing page, a one-vs-one page and a free-only roundup that leaves Showthrough out. Turn /alternatives into the roundup, make /alternatives/loom a "Showthrough vs Loom" page for pay-once searches, and keep /blog/free-loom-alternatives about free tools only.

  2. 2

    The homepage is slow on mobile

    Lab LCP is 5.0 s and the browser is blocked for 2.6 s; Lighthouse score 52. The server answers in about 100 ms, so the cause is JavaScript: PostHog session recording, surveys and dead-click tracking on load, the ship-or-die.com/hackathon/verify.js script, and hydration delaying the hero text.

  3. 3

    Two claims are wrong or premature

    The setup guide says 10 GB holds "roughly 100 hours". The extension records at 2.5 Mbps video plus 128 kbps audio, about 1.2 GB an hour, so it's about 8 hours (components/guide-pages.tsx:65). The site also promises "one payment for your whole team" while team features are listed as coming soon.

  4. 4

    Few reasons to trust the site yet

    No About or author page, support on a Gmail address, no reviews, ratings or testimonials, no demo video, and the brand's sameAs lists only X and the Chrome Web Store.

  5. 5

    The mobile nav is broken

    At 375 px the "Get Showthrough" button runs off the screen edge, and the nav links are hidden below 1024 px with no menu button (components/site-nav.tsx:30). Google still sees the links in the HTML, so this hurts conversions rather than rankings.

What slows the homepage

  • The slowest element on mobile is the hero paragraph, held back 1.5 s by script work. The preloaded dashboard image isn't the slow part, so that preload doesn't help on mobile.
  • PostHog loads its recorder (74 KB), surveys (48 KB) and dead-click tracking on page load.
  • About 195 KB of unused JavaScript and 32 KB of legacy transpilation.
  • The Newsreader variable font is 129 KB; a subset would be smaller.
